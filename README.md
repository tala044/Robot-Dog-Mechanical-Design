# Robot Dog Mechanical Design

A simple mechanical design of a four-legged robotic dog created using Tinkercad. This project focuses on understanding the basic mechanical structure required for a robot to stand and walk.

## Project Overview

The robotic dog design includes:

- Main body structure
- Four mechanical legs
- Upper and lower leg segments
- Hip and knee joints
- Basic joint movement concept
- Servo motor placeholders
- Mechanical stability considerations
- Center of gravity considerations
- A basic walking concept
- Possible mechanical challenges

The goal of this project is not to build an advanced robot, but to demonstrate an understanding of the basic mechanical principles behind a legged robot.

## Design Components

### Body

The main body acts as the central structure of the robot and provides space for the electronic components, such as:

- Arduino controller
- Battery
- Other control components

### Legs

The robot has four legs positioned around the body. Each leg contains:

1. Upper leg segment
2. Lower leg segment
3. Hip joint
4. Knee joint
5. Foot

The legs are designed to provide basic support and allow simple movement.

### Joints and Movement

Each leg uses mechanical joints to represent the movement points of the robotic dog. The joints allow the leg segments to move and demonstrate how servo motors could control the robot's movement.

The expected movement includes:

- Hip rotation
- Knee bending
- Forward and backward leg movement

## Servo Motor Selection

Servo motors are considered for controlling the robot joints because they can provide controlled rotational movement.

The servo motor placeholders in the design represent the locations where motors could be installed to control the hip and knee joints.

## Mechanical Calculations

Basic mechanical calculations are included to estimate the torque required at the joints and to consider the effect of the robot's weight and center of gravity.

These calculations help determine an appropriate servo motor torque for supporting and moving the legs.

See the following file for the calculations:

- `Mechanical_Calculations.md`

## Stability and Center of Gravity

The robot's stability depends on the position of its center of gravity and the placement of the four legs.

A stable design should:

- Keep the center of gravity close to the center of the body
- Maintain a wide enough support area between the legs
- Distribute the weight across the four legs
- Avoid excessive weight on one side of the robot

## Basic Walking Concept

A simple walking cycle can be achieved by moving the legs in sequence.

For example:

1. Move one front leg forward
2. Move the opposite rear leg forward
3. Shift the robot's weight to maintain balance
4. Repeat the movement with the other pair of legs

This basic concept can later be developed into a more advanced walking gait.

## Potential Mechanical Challenges

Possible challenges include:

- Maintaining robot balance
- Selecting motors with sufficient torque
- Preventing joint interference
- Supporting the weight of the body and electronics
- Coordinating the movement of all four legs
- Maintaining stability during walking

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates the basic mechanical design principles of a four-legged robotic dog. The final design includes a body, four legs, mechanical joints, and servo motor positions.

The project provides a foundation for understanding robotic mechanisms, joint movement, stability, torque requirements, and the basic principles required for a robot to stand and walk.
