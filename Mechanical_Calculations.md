# Robot Dog Mechanical Design

A simple mechanical design of a robotic dog created using Tinkercad as part of a robotics and mechanical design task.

## Project Overview

The goal of this project is to design a simple robotic dog and demonstrate the basic mechanical principles that allow a robot to stand and move.

The design includes:

- Robot body and frame
- Four mechanical legs
- Hip and knee joints
- Joint movement considerations
- Center of gravity and stability considerations
- Basic actuator placement
- Simple walking mechanism concept
- Mechanical torque calculations

## Mechanical Design

The robotic dog consists of a rectangular main body with four legs attached underneath.

Each leg includes:

- Upper leg segment
- Lower leg segment
- Mechanical joints between the body and leg segments
- Foot section for contact with the ground

The legs are positioned to provide a stable base and support the robot's body.

## Joints and Movement

The mechanical design includes joints that represent the movement points of the robotic dog.

The joints allow the legs to move and demonstrate how a robotic leg can be connected using mechanical components and actuators such as servo motors.

## Actuators

Servo motors are considered as the main actuators for controlling the joints.

The actuator placement is represented in the mechanical design to demonstrate where motors could be installed to control the movement of the legs.

## Stability and Center of Gravity

The robot body is positioned above the four legs to create a stable structure.

The center of gravity should remain within the support area created by the feet to help the robot maintain balance while standing and moving.

## Walking Mechanism

A simple walking concept can be achieved by moving the legs in an alternating sequence.

For example, diagonal legs can move together while the other pair supports the body.

## Mechanical Calculations

Basic mechanical calculations are included in the `Mechanical_Calculations.md` file.

These calculations demonstrate the basic torque requirements for a robotic leg joint.

The basic torque equation used is:

T = F × L

Where:

- T = Required torque
- F = Applied force
- L = Distance from the joint

## Conclusion

This project demonstrates the basic mechanical structure of a robotic dog.

The focus of the project is understanding the relationship between the body, legs, joints, actuators, torque, stability, and walking movement rather than building a fully advanced robotic dog.
